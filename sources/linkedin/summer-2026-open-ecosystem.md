## Copy-ready LinkedIn post

What an amazing week in San Francisco.

I was at ModCon when Modular open-sourced the Mojo compiler, one week after the release of Mojo 1.0. At a moment when AI dominates the developer conversation, the bigger story is not any single announcement. It is the convergence of four developments across four layers of the open software stack:

1. **Mojo 1.0 and an open compiler.** Modular released Mojo 1.0 and then opened the compiler and toolchain at ModCon. The company aims to begin accepting external compiler and tooling contributions by the end of the year.

2. **DeepSeek Harness.** DeepSeek released an MIT-licensed developer preview of its agent harness, built on Cordis. Its plugin architecture, typed events, reversible effects, and traceable execution make the harness itself open to inspection and extension.

3. **Lean FRO and CSLib.** Amazon's Automated Reasoning organization made the largest donation in Lean FRO's history. Separately, CSLib is building shared computer-science libraries and verification infrastructure on Lean.

4. **The open-weights letter.** A broad industry coalition—including major technology companies—called for continued support for open-weight models and the ecosystem around them.

Any one of these developments would matter. Together, they are far more powerful: open models, open harnesses, open compilers, and open proof infrastructure can give independent researchers and developers leverage that previously required a large institution.

## Editorial and factual notes

- Replaced “there is only one game in town—AI” with a more credible statement that AI dominates the current developer conversation.
- Kept the central insight: the combination matters more than any individual announcement.
- Did not say MAX will become open source by year end. Modular's public statement is that it aims to accept contributions to the Mojo compiler and tooling by year end.
- Described Cordis through claims supported by its documentation—typed events, reversible effects, plugin composition, and traceability—without claiming that DeepSeek Harness itself has been formally verified.
- Kept Amazon's Lean FRO donation separate from CSLib. They are related parts of the proof ecosystem, but the donation announcement does not say that the funding flows from Lean FRO to CSLib.
- Replaced “open pledge” with “open-weights letter.” The letter advocates for open weights; it is not a binding pledge by every signatory.
